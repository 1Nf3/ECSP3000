\section{Rootkits: Definition \& Context}

Rootkits are typically described as a specialised piece of malware built for persistence \& stealth which is installed \textit{after} an attacker has gained root-level access to a system \citep{rootkitNist}.
They may also give an attacker additional functionality such as network backdoors (cite magic bpf), the ability to hide files or other capabilities that give the attacker a greater ability to hide their presence whilst maintaining persistence.

Linux \& subsequently Linux rootkits have been chosen for this project due to its relevance and prevalence in cloud \& server environments. Additionally, a wide range of rootkit techniques exist for Linux which will be covered in the following section.
Consequently, many tools have been developed which aim to detect the presence of a rootkit on a system. To achieve this, these tools may take a variety of approaches which will be covered in section 1.3.
